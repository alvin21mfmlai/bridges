1Basic Analytics

CS1 → CS2: time in the best condition state (Prediction Method #1)

How long does the element stay in CS1 (good) before moving to CS2?

Each dot is the share of the element still in CS1 for bridges in one 5-year age band. Bigger dots mean more bridges.

The blue line is the prediction curve.

Observed share in CS1Prediction curve

How this is calculated

  • Data: the selected state, period and element type. Each bridge counts once, using its most recent inspection in the period. Bridges with no element quantity, deck area or age, a failed quantity check, or an age outside 1 to 100 years are left out.
  • Observed share in CS1: bridges are grouped into 5-year age bands. For each band, share in CS1 = total CS1 quantity ÷ total element quantity (CS1 to CS4). Each band is plotted at its midpoint age (3, 8 … 98 years).
  • Model: S(t) = exp(−(t/η)β), the expected share of the element quantity still in CS1 at age t. It assumes the element is all in CS1 when new.
    • Shape β: above 1, the element leaves CS1 faster as it ages. Below 1, it leaves more slowly, and about 1 means a steady rate.
    • Scale η: the age by which 63.2% of the quantity has left CS1.
  • Fitting: β and η are estimated by maximum likelihood (binomial), treating each band's share as the part that has not yet left CS1 by that age. Each band is weighted by its number of bridges.
  • Results: median time in CS1 = η·(ln 2)1/β, and mean time in CS1 = η·Γ(1 + 1/β). The fit error is the weighted root-mean-square difference between observed and fitted shares.
  • Limitations: each bridge is seen once, so the curve compares bridges of different ages rather than following one bridge over time. Repairs return quantity to CS1 and flatten the curve at older ages. Age bands with few bridges make the ends of the curve less certain.

2Basic Analytics

CS2 → CS3 → CS4: movement to worse condition states (Prediction Method #2)

How quickly does the element move from CS2 to CS3, and from CS3 to CS4?

Each line is the yearly chance that part of the element moves to the next condition state.

CS1 → CS2 comes from Prediction Method #1. CS2 → CS3 and CS3 → CS4 are fitted for each 20-year age group.

Yearly chance of moving to the next state, by age group

The chance of staying in the current state is 100% minus the value.

Transition probability matrix

Rows: this year's condition state. Columns: next year's. Each row adds up to 100%.

Shaded cells are moves to a worse state.

How well the model matches the data

Lines: shares predicted by the model, starting fully in CS1 when new. Dots: observed shares in each 5-year age band.

How this is calculated

  • Data: the same bridges as Prediction Method #1 above. For each 5-year age band, the observed share in each condition state = total quantity in that state ÷ total element quantity.
  • Model: a yearly step-by-step model of the share of the element in CS1 to CS4. Each year a fraction of CS1 moves to CS2, of CS2 to CS3, and of CS3 to CS4. Nothing moves back, and CS4 stays.
    • CS1 → CS2 uses the Prediction Method #1 curve above: the yearly chance at age a is 1 − S(a+1) ÷ S(a). The table shows its average over each age category.
    • CS2 → CS3 and CS3 → CS4 have one yearly chance per 20-year age category (1–20, 21–40, 41–60, 61–80, 81–100), giving one transition probability matrix per category.
  • Fitting: starting with all of the element in CS1 when new, the model is run year by year to age 100. The CS2 → CS3 and CS3 → CS4 chances are chosen by maximum likelihood (multinomial) so the predicted mix matches the observed mix in each age band, each band weighted by its number of bridges. A small penalty on jumps between neighbouring categories keeps thinly populated categories stable.
  • Fit error: weighted root-mean-square difference between the observed and predicted shares in CS1 to CS4.
  • Limitations: each bridge is seen once, so the chances are inferred from bridges of different ages rather than from the same bridge over time. Repairs move quantity back to better states, which the model does not include, so its chances are net of repairs. Where little of the element has reached CS3 or CS4, those chances rest on thin data.

1Basic Analytics (Bridge Level)

Health score by data year

How healthy are this state's bridges, and is that changing?

Each bridge gets a health score from 0% to 100%, from every element type on the bridge, weighted by each element's quantity × unit cost. You can change the unit costs above the map. The defaults are placeholders: $40 per sq ft, $1,200 per ft and $16,000 each. A score of 100% means all of its elements are in CS1 (good), and 0% means all of them are in CS4 (severe).
